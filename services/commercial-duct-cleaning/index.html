Commercial Duct Cleaning Woodstock, ON

Commercial ventilation scope varies with the building, operating schedule, and equipment included. For a Woodstock office, clinic, daycare, restaurant, or industrial space, gather system drawings if available and list the air-handling units, duct sections, and make-up air equipment under consideration. Ask for a written scope that identifies access requirements, work sequencing, operating constraints, and documentation. This helps property managers compare proposals and decide whether work can be coordinated around occupants, tenants, and essential building operations.

Questions About the Work

Are occupants reporting stale or uneven airflow?

Complaints can relate to filters, controls, ventilation rates, or duct condition. Record affected areas and equipment schedules so an assessment can identify which systems need review.

  • Plan Work Around Building Operations

    Share occupancy hours, restricted areas, and equipment shutdown limits before requesting a plan. If work outside normal hours is important, ask whether that timing can be arranged and how access, noise, and coordination with other contractors will be handled.

  • Request Scope and Completion Records

    Ask for a written description of areas and equipment included, along with photographs or a completion record where available. Clear documentation helps a manager compare the planned work with the completed scope and retain information for building maintenance files.

  • Include Make-Up Air and Rooftop Equipment

    If the building has make-up air units or rooftop equipment, identify them separately and ask how they will be assessed. Access, equipment configuration, and operating requirements can change the scope; confirm which components are included before comparing proposals.
